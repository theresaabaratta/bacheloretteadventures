Water Sports

Charleston Water Sports for a Bachelorette Party

By Bachelorette AdventuresUpdated November 20265 Min. Read
Photo via Pexels

Charleston is surrounded by water, and the easiest way to give a bachelorette weekend some fresh air is to get the whole group out on it.

Kayak and Paddleboard the Tidal Creeks

Shem Creek in Mount Pleasant is the classic launch point. Nature Adventures Outfitters runs guided kayak and paddleboard trips through the creek and surrounding marsh, and dolphins are a regular sighting. Paddle back in and the creek's waterfront restaurants are a few steps from the dock, which makes it an easy morning-into-lunch plan.

On the Folly side, Charleston Outdoor Adventures leads kayak and paddleboard tours on the Folly River with rentals for groups who want to go out on their own. Coastal Expeditions is the other big name, with naturalist-led paddles and private trips that can be built around a bridal party.

Surf Lessons at Folly Beach

Folly Beach is Charleston's surf town, and the waves are friendly enough for a group of first-timers. Book a group lesson with one of the local surf schools, or rent boards from McKevlin's Surf Shop, which has been on Center Street since the 1960s. The Washout on the east end is where the locals go; beginners do better near the pier on a small day.

Sailing and Harbor Cruises

For a slower pace, a sunset sail on the Schooner Pride puts the group on a tall ship crossing Charleston Harbor with the Battery and Ravenel Bridge in view. SpiritLine Cruises runs harbor tours and the ferry to Fort Sumter, a good pick if part of the crew wants some history with the boat ride.

Rent a Boat for the Day

Groups that want the water to themselves can rent a pontoon or center console from the Isle of Palms Marina and spend the day anchored off a sandbar in the Intracoastal. A licensed captain is the smart move for a bachelorette crowd, and most charters around Isle of Palms and Shem Creek offer one.

Timing the Water

Late April through October is the warm-water season. Morning trips beat the summer heat and afternoon storms, and tide times matter for creek paddling, so book with an outfitter who plans around them. Keep the drinking for after the boat is tied up.
